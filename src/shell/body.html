Jet lag planner

A light-exposure schedule for moving your body clock. The direction matters more than the amount — light at the wrong hour shifts the clock the wrong way, so the timings below are worked out against your own sleep pattern rather than a generic table.

Turbulence across an area

The same physics on a grid instead of along a route. Pan and zoom to the area you care about, then load it — the field is fetched for the visible view on demand, because a live global grid is not affordable on a free API tier and pretending otherwise would just fail.

pan the map to see the cost

Live traffic is not here, and cannot be from a static page. Aircraft visibly routing around a red patch would be the forecast validating itself in public, and it is the feature this view most wants. Every free ADS-B source refuses browser origins: OpenSky answers other origins with a restrictive access-control-allow-origin, adsb.lol sends no CORS header, and airplanes.live returns 403. All three work from a server. The Worker in server/ carries the proxy route that unblocks this — it is a deployment away, not a code change.

The air in three dimensions

A raymarch through the field the briefing already computed — turbulence as volumetric fog on the same EDR colour scale, the jet stream as a 30 m/s isosurface, the tropopause, the terrain below and the flight path threading through it. Drag to orbit, scroll to zoom.

The cross-track axis carries no turbulence information. The analysis samples two points either side of track, but spends them on the horizontal wind gradients Ellrod's index needs — it never computes a separate EDR there. So the corridor is drawn as a slab of constant cross-track value: real depth and parallax, no cross-track detail. Along-track and vertical are both genuinely resolved, one column per waypoint across the ten pressure levels. Resolving the third axis means fetching a grid instead of a line, which is a request-count decision rather than a rendering one.

Record this flight

Lay the phone flat on the tray table and start. It samples the accelerometer, fits the Kolmogorov −5/3 slope to each ten-second window, and keeps a relative roughness index. Windows where the phone was picked up, tapped or walked past are discarded rather than scored. Everything stays on this device.

Best time to fly

The same forecast run across every departure slot for the next week, so you can see the ride before you book rather than after. The week is fetched once and re-sampled per slot — the fields do not change with your departure time, only the moment you meet them.

Seeing forecast

Astronomical seeing is dominated by turbulence near the jet stream — the same shear this site already computes for aircraft. This points that engine at the ground instead of a flight path and reports tonight's free-atmosphere seeing hour by hour.

How this forecast is built, and where it comes from

Read this as a planning aid, not an operational product. Ride Report is built from public numerical weather model data and standard clear-air-turbulence diagnostics. It is not a flight-planning tool, it is not certified for aviation use, and it has no access to your actual filed route, assigned flight levels, or the dispatcher's turbulence forecast. Crews get better data than this. If a captain says sit down, sit down.
